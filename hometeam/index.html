HomeTeam · my own family

The application my family runs the week on. My children voted on how it looks.

HomeTeam keeps my family's week in one place. The children see what needs doing today and what it earns them, the adults see the whole week, and the children's money works like money: a weekly allowance, a loan to pay back, a goal to save for. I built it for my own family, and the family tried the first designs on their own phones and voted on them.

In use · my own family · 2026 →

What it does

Today is the first screen. A child sees the day's tasks as large cards: a tap and a confirmation mark one done, and its coins go straight to the child's wallet. A parent sees how far each child has got and what is waiting for an adult. The calendar holds the family's week with week numbers, and an entry a child adds waits for a parent's approval.

Each family sets its own economy: coins with a euro value or plain euros, a weekly allowance, a loan paid back from earnings, rewards to save for and a savings goal. On top sits a game layer the family can switch on: a weekly scoreboard where the adults' housework counts too, a treasure island that rises from the sand as tasks get done, and a wheel to spin once a day.

Why it exists

HomeTeam replaced an earlier version my family used. Two lessons from it shaped this one. The children lost interest in collecting points after about two months, so this version rests on things that keep their value: real money, a shared goal and content that changes. And small lists and icons made the old one heavy to use, so here a routine task takes two taps, and every screen starts as a mockup.

The first designs went to the family as clickable prototypes on their own phones, and everyone voted, the children included.

Where it stands

In use by my family since 29 September 2026. Only the addresses I have allowed can sign in, so there is no public way in, and the code is in a private repository. The family's data is kept in the EU. The application is in Finnish.

Typefaces in the mockups: Bangers, Gochi Hand, Patrick Hand (SIL Open Font License). 3D images: Microsoft Fluent Emoji (MIT).
